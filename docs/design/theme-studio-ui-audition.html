The Studio does a lot, and it's grown one feature at a time. This page takes a step back: what gets in the way today, three ways to lay the whole window out, and ten smaller decisions about controls and flow. The mockups below work: click the tabs, drag the sliders, pick colors and themes. Choose a layout and an option for each decision, then copy your picks back to me.

What gets in the way today

Screens from the current version, and what a designer would flag on them.

  1. The tabs look like the options under them. Colors, Type, Glass and Agents are the same bordered pills as Block, Bar or Compact, so it's hard to tell where you are from what you can pick.
  2. Saving shows up in two places. There's a Save bar under the preview and a footer with Revert under that. Discard and Revert sound like the same thing but do very different things.
  3. Every section ends in a gray paragraph. The help is useful once. After that, it's most of what's on screen and pushes the controls apart.
  4. Each slider takes two lines, label above and bar below. The Agents tab has 12 of them and scrolls a long way.
  5. The theme bar has five buttons that all look the same. Delete sits right next to Apply, and Copy and Copy JSON look as important as Edit.
  6. Herdr's themes need a Copy step and a name before you can change anything.
  7. Tools you rarely need sit in the main flow. Revert, the palette icon and Show themes folder share the screen with everyday choices.
  8. Colors use code names like terminal_foreground, and the picker opens inside the list, pushing everything under it down.
  9. Glass presets look like choices but are one-time actions. Nothing shows which preset you're on.
